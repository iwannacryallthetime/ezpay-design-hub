ezPay design system

Tokens and components shared by the AI checker and the requestor form. Link tokens.css and components.css, put class="ez" on the page, and use the classes below. differences.md lists where the prototypes don't match yet.

  • Semantic tokens onlyComponents use aliases like --text-muted, never a palette step or hex code.
  • Sentence caseNo all-caps or Title Case copy. Acronyms (GST, SGD, AOR, PDF) stay as they are.
  • Colour plus a signalEvery status pairs its colour with an icon or words, so it reads without colour.
  • Real buttons, visible focusControls are <button>s with a teal focus ring.

Brand roles

Four palette colours carry a fixed job. Components reach them through semantic aliases.

--brandIdentity colour: the brand bar, active workspace and nav item, total banner. Not for buttons or links.
--brand-darkSelection accent in navigation: the selected tab's label and underline.
--actionPrimary actions: the fill of primary buttons and the outline of secondary ones.
--dangerDestructive decisions (Reject, Discard). Text and outline only.

Type

Inter, with a system fallback. Body copy is sm (12px). Weights: 400 regular, 500 medium, 600 semibold, 700 bold. Amounts in boxes and inputs use the mono stack.

Spacing and sizes

4px base. There's no 10px step: gaps and control padding use 8px, and row heights come from size tokens. Figma paddings like 9, 11 or 13px are a step plus a 1px border.

Buttons

One primary per screen, for the main decision. Secondary for the other actions beside it. Danger for destructive or negative decisions; ask for confirmation. Plain for low-emphasis actions in dialogs and footers. Accent for compact actions inside AI checker panels. Labels say what happens: "Submit claim", not "OK".

Cards and section headers

A card groups one section of a form; its header carries a numbered section title and, on the right, meta text or one link. Line items use a compact item row when closed and an item card when open, one open at a time.

Navigation

Tabs switch between views of the same record. A stepper splits one task into ordered steps; you can always go back, and only go forward once earlier steps are complete. The sidebar is the app's main navigation. The action bar holds the screen's decisions and stays pinned to the bottom.

Dialogs and toasts

Use a dialog to confirm a decision that's hard to undo, or to collect one more detail. The title says what will happen ("Reject this claim"), and the confirm button repeats it. Use a toast to confirm something that already happened; it disappears on its own, so never put the only copy of important information in it.

AI checker patterns

Used wherever AI checks a claim. The teal panel header frames the AI's work; purple marks anything the AI produced (the AI tag, AI-filled status). Show the AI's evidence next to the claim's value so the checker can decide, and always let them override it.
